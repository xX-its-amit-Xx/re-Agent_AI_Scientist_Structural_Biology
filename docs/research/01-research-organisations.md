# How productive research organisations actually structured themselves

Evidence grades: **A** controlled or meta-analytic · **B** documented primary/archival fact ·
**C** scholarly inference or self-report · **D** folklore.

Two structural caveats apply to everything below and should be read first. This
literature **selects on the dependent variable**: Janelia was designed by intersecting
the features of three survivors, and the failed labs sharing those features were never
enumerated. And two of the three canonical laboratories were **monopoly-funded**, which
is a confound no amount of organisational insight substitutes for. No independent
bibliometric evaluation of Janelia or the Institute for Advanced Study exists.

## Claims that did not survive checking

These matter more than the confirmations, because they are the ones a designer would
otherwise build on.

**Bell Labs' "no closed doors" rule is not documented (D).** The phrase traces to a book
reviewer's paraphrase; a full-text sweep finds no primary formulation. Hamming, who
worked there thirty years, described a *correlation* between open doors and productivity
and then wrote plainly: *"I cannot prove the cause and effect sequence."* Shannon — the
usual poster child for corridor serendipity — **kept his door closed**, and Gertner
describes him as "a model of a lone genius which was fairly unusual at Bell Labs."

**Xerox PARC deliberately chose the opposite arrangement, and its evidence is better
(B).** Taylor gave researchers acoustically private walled offices with glass, plus a
*mandatory weekly all-hands*. That is scheduled collision with protected focus — not
ambient collision. The two most-cited laboratories disagree on this point, and the one
that disagrees with the folklore is the better-documented one.

**An assigned critic is the weak design (A).** Nemeth, Brown & Rogers (2001) found
authentic minority dissent *"superior to all three forms of 'devil's advocate'"*, and
that assigned advocacy produced **cognitive bolstering of the initial view** — it made
people more confident in what they already thought. PARC got civil, sharp criticism from
*hiring selection*, not from facilitation technique.

**The "small teams disrupt, large teams develop" magnitudes are unsafe (A).** Petersen
et al. (2024) show the CD index is biased by citation inflation and warn explicitly that
this "stymies efforts to correlate disruption to other measures that are also time
dependent, such as team size." The *direction* survives; the numbers do not.

**Dunbar's number is refuted, not merely contested (A).** Reanalysis gives confidence
intervals spanning roughly 4 to 520. Do not use it to justify a team-size cap.

Also unsupported on checking: Genentech's internal horse-race, Rathmann personally saving
the erythropoietin project, a "fifteenth rule" of Kelly Johnson's, the Skunk Works "50
feet" story, Oppenheimer's "intellectual hotel", Yang–Mills at the IAS, and the Bell Labs
"$1 per patent".

## Organisation by organisation

**Bell Labs (1925–1980s).** Genuine mechanisms: co-location of theorists,
experimentalists and manufacturing engineers in one institution, so a physical result had
a path to production; long time horizons underwritten by monopoly revenue; and promotion
decided by local technical judgement. Hamming's *You and Your Research* is the primary
document worth reading, and its actual central claim is about **problem selection** — that
the difference between people of comparable ability is whether they work on important
problems, and that this requires deliberately asking what the important problems in the
field are. Its causal claims about open doors are explicitly hedged by its author.

**MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, Cambridge.** The highest Nobel-per-researcher
rate on record. Documented features: small groups; no formal hierarchy between group
leaders; a shared canteen where the day's results were discussed before they were
written; and — the load-bearing one — **funding and evaluation attached to people rather
than projects**. Brenner: *"We never let the committee assess individuals."* Perutz's
management was minimal by design.

**Xerox PARC.** The Dealer meeting: a weekly forum where one person presented and
everyone else attacked the idea, with the norm that the attack was on the work and not
the person, sustained by who was hired rather than by a facilitator. Private offices for
focus; one mandatory collision point per week.

**Lockheed Skunk Works.** Kelly Johnson's fourteen rules are a real primary document.
The transferable content is authority delegation (the programme manager had practical
control over specification, budget and schedule), radical reduction in reporting
overhead, small co-located teams, and a deliberately restricted number of external
interfaces.

**The Manhattan Project.** The instructive tension is internal: Groves imposed
compartmentalisation across the project, and Oppenheimer insisted on the **Los Alamos
colloquium**, where the scientific problem was discussed openly across the site. The
documented view of participants is that the colloquium was necessary for the physics.
Compartmentalisation and scientific progress were in direct conflict, and one was
suspended locally to get the other.

> **Do not cite this in support of our isolation discipline.** Read plainly, Los Alamos is
> evidence **against** compartmentalisation: the people doing the physics thought it was
> obstructing them, and it was locally abandoned. Our design nonetheless isolates workers
> and funnels them into one synthesis point, and it should be justified by the measured
> correlated-error results in [`04`](04-agent-architectures.md) — independent aggregation at
> 83.43% against deliberative consensus at 76.11% — not by this episode. The two arrangements
> look alike and rest on opposite evidence. The difference is that Oppenheimer's physicists
> had genuinely different private information to pool, and same-family agents do not.

**Janelia Research Campus.** Explicitly modelled on the LMB and Bell Labs. What it
copied: small groups with hard caps, no tenure, shared instrumentation staff, internal
funding removing grant-writing overhead. What it changed: it made group-size limits
formal rather than cultural.

**The Institute for Advanced Study.** Included because the critique is instructive: a
concentration of extraordinary individuals, minimal structure, no experimental
apparatus, and an output that many assessments regard as below what the inputs implied.
Whatever the successful labs were doing, "hire brilliant people and leave them alone"
does not appear to be sufficient.

**CERN and large collaborations.** The mechanism worth noting is that coordination cost
is paid *once*, in the instrument and the data format, and thereafter working groups
operate semi-independently against a shared artifact. Authorship conventions distribute
credit in a way that makes contribution to shared infrastructure rational.

**Industrial biotech (Genentech, early Amgen).** Thirteen oral histories were read in
full. The recurring documented mechanism is that scientific publication was permitted
and encouraged, which kept the recruitment and criticism channels of academia open
inside a commercial organisation.

## Cross-cutting mechanisms that recur in the successes

**Evaluate the person, not the project.** The single mechanism every successful
organisation converges on: local judgement of individuals by people who understand the
work, and refusal of remote per-project review. This is the most consistent finding in
the entire investigation.

**Long horizons, deliberately.** Four separate literatures converge on this: novel work
needs windows of nine years or more to show its citation advantage; a randomised
grant-review trial found a novelty coefficient of **−2.67** that was demonstrable *bias*
rather than noise; HHMI's investigator-funding advantage does not appear for three years.
Short-horizon evaluation systematically selects against the work you most want.

**Criticism institutionalised through selection, not procedure.** PARC's Dealer worked
because of who was in the room. The formal-role literature (see
[`03-inquiry-methodologies.md`](03-inquiry-methodologies.md)) independently finds that
*assigned* dissent underperforms authentic dissent.

**One scheduled collision point, plus protected focus.** Taylor's arrangement, the LMB
canteen, the Los Alamos colloquium. Not ambient interruption.

**Reduced reporting overhead as a first-class design goal.** Skunk Works and Janelia both
treat the removal of grant-writing and status-reporting as a productivity intervention
rather than an administrative convenience.

## What preceded the declines

Bell Labs and PARC both declined after the same two changes: the horizon over which
work was evaluated shortened, and the path from research to production was cut — at PARC
by the parent company's inability to commercialise, at Bell Labs by divestiture removing
the monopoly rent that funded long horizons. In both cases the *structure* of the
research group was intact when its output fell. The organisational features people
credit were not what changed.

## The scholarship

**Team size and impact.** Wuchty, Jones & Uzzi (*Science*, 2007) on the shift to team
production. Wu, Wang & Evans (*Nature*, 2019) on small teams disrupting and large teams
developing — direction reliable, magnitudes compromised by the CD-index bias above.

**Novelty and atypicality.** Uzzi et al. (*Science* 342, 2013, 468–72): the
highest-impact work is *"primarily grounded in exceptionally conventional combinations of
prior work yet simultaneously features an intrusion of unusual combinations"* — roughly
double the odds of high impact, with teams 37.7 % more likely to supply the unusual
element. **A conventional core with an atypical tail, not maximised novelty.**

**Evaluator quality dominates search breadth.** Scannell & Bosley: in a screening
pipeline, a **0.1 change in the correlation between the evaluator and ground truth "can
offset large (e.g., 10 fold, even 100 fold) changes in brute-force efficiency."** This is
the most directly actionable quantitative finding in the whole investigation.

**Burden of knowledge.** Jones on the rising age at first invention and narrowing
individual breadth, which is the structural argument for why the connector role matters
and why it is getting harder.

## Transferable principles

The full report enumerates 28. The ones that survive contact with a multi-agent system:

**P1 — Invest in the scorer before the sampler.** Widening a candidate pool only helps up
to the discrimination of the thing selecting from it. Quantified above.

**P11 — Any short-horizon evaluator will kill your best work.** If a loop selects on a
fast proxy, it reproduces the −2.67 novelty penalty exactly.

**P12 — Evaluate the agent, not the task.** Track which worker or configuration produces
claims that survive verification, and steer compute on that, rather than scoring
individual outputs in isolation.

**P14 — Decorrelate by construction, not by instruction.** Different models, corpora and
objectives; never different personas. Confirmed independently in
[`02`](02-individual-methods.md) and [`03`](03-inquiry-methodologies.md).

**P19 — One scheduled synthesis point; isolation the rest of the time.** The
Taylor arrangement, translated: workers do not see each other's output during
production, and there is exactly one place where everything is brought together.

**P22 — Remove reporting overhead from the workers.** A worker that must summarise its
own work for an orchestrator spends capacity on presentation. Prefer a structured return
schema the worker fills mechanically.

**P26 — Keep the path from finding to use short.** The decline mechanism at both Bell
Labs and PARC was the severing of research from application, not a change in how research
was organised.
